## Key observations

- **Omega-3 Index rose** from 4.93% to 6.06% in the two same-lab OmegaQuant panels. The commonly cited 8–12% range is a proposed cardiovascular risk range, not a universal treatment or obesity target.
- **AA:EPA ratio was high relative to the reporting lab's range.** The 2019 result used a different lab/method, so the strongest comparison is 2024→2025.
- **n6:n3 ratio fell** in the available panels. The ratio itself is not an established obesity-treatment target.
- **Linoleic acid trending down** (26.9 → 25.0 → 22.9) — possible dietary oil changes.
- **Palmitoleic acid halved** (0.92 → 0.53) between 2024–2025. It is influenced by de novo lipogenesis and metabolic state but is not a specific standalone excess-carbohydrate marker.
- **Trans fat index low and dropping** (0.67 → 0.44) — consistent with no meat and limited processed food.
- **AA rose** from 2024→2025 (7.52→8.93) while LA fell. This is compatible with the FADS profile but does not prove increased conversion caused the change; intake, supplementation, turnover, and assay variation remain alternatives.

## Context for omega-6:3 theories

FADS1 rs174546 CC is associated with higher desaturase measures and can affect fatty-acid profiles. The observed issue is simpler: EPA was low relative to AA under the laboratory's ratio. This does not demonstrate systemic inflammation, prove that dietary linoleic acid is harmful, or identify a cause of obesity. Randomized-trial reviews generally do not find increased inflammatory markers when linoleic acid intake rises.

## Supplementation timeline

- **2025-08-17**: Started daily 500mg EPA + 500mg DHA (algal oil). The 2025-09-05 OmegaQuant test was only 20 days later—too early for full RBC steady state, but not too early for any incorporation. The 2024→2025 change cannot be assigned to diet alone. A same-lab draw after at least 16 weeks of a stable verified dose can test uptake, not a weight-loss mechanism.
